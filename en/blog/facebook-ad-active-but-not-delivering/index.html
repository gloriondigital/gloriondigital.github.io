Facebook ad active but not delivering? 7 causes and fixes

Short answer: If your Meta ad shows as active but gets no impressions, start with the Delivery column: the ad may be in review, rejected or restricted. If not, check in order your payment method, whether the budget is enough, whether the audience is too narrow, your bid cap, and the schedule and on/off status at campaign, ad set and ad level.

“The ad is live, it says active, but it hasn't had a single impression all day.” We get this message several times a week. An ad that gets impressions but no messages is a different problem, covered in our guide on why ads get impressions but no messages. This one is about the step before that: an ad that isn't delivering at all, or barely.

How can you tell whether your ad is really running?

In Ads Manager, the campaign, ad set and ad each have their own on/off switch, and all three must be on for the ad to run. Then read the Delivery column at each level: labels such as “In review”, “Rejected”, “Learning limited” or “Budget too low” usually point straight at the problem. Meta also has a dedicated troubleshooting guide for campaigns that slow down or stop (Meta Business Help Center).

1. The ad is still in review

Every ad is reviewed against Meta's standards before it runs, and its status shows “In review” in the meantime (Meta Business Help Center). Review times vary. Editing the image, copy or targeting can send the ad back into review, so avoid rushed changes.

2. The ad was rejected or the account is restricted

Ads that don't meet the Meta Advertising Standards are rejected and won't run. The reason appears next to the ad and in Account Quality. If you think the decision is wrong, you can request another review; Meta explains how it enforces ad policies and how appeals work (Meta Business Help Center).

3. There's a payment problem

If the card was declined or there's an unpaid balance, ads stop. Check Billing for warnings and confirm with your bank that the card allows online and international payments.

4. The budget is too low for the result you chose

Meta shows a “Budget too low” warning when the budget isn't enough for the optimization goal (Meta Business Help Center). Splitting a small budget across many ad sets makes this worse, so try merging them first.

5. The audience is too narrow

A tiny location, stacked interests and long exclusion lists can leave almost no one to show the ad to. For under-delivering campaigns, Meta suggests broadening the audience (Meta Business Help Center). We show how to balance this for a local business in our guide to advertising a preschool to parents.

6. The bid cap or cost goal is too low

If you use a bid cap and it sits below what it takes to win the auction, the ad may not be shown. Meta publishes best practices for bid caps (Meta Business Help Center). If you're unsure, start with the lowest cost strategy.

7. The schedule, or one level is switched off

The start date may be set in the future, the end date may have passed, or the ad schedule may be limited to certain hours. If the campaign is on but the ad set is off, the ad won't run either.

A step-by-step checklist

  1. Confirm the campaign, ad set and ad are all switched on.
  2. Read the Delivery column at each level and hover over any warning.
  3. Check Account Quality for rejections or restrictions.
  4. Look for payment warnings in Billing.
  5. Review the budget and the number of ad sets.
  6. Check the audience definition, location radius and exclusions.
  7. Check the bid strategy and the dates.

What if the ad is running but gets very few impressions?

If the ad set says “Learning limited”, Meta is telling you it isn't getting enough results to stabilize delivery (Meta Business Help Center). The usual fixes are merging ad sets, broadening the audience or optimizing for a result that happens more often. Frequent small edits can also restart learning.

Why isn't my boosted post delivering?

Boosted posts follow the same review, payment and budget rules, but you get fewer tools to see what's wrong. If it keeps happening, read our comparison of Boost vs Ads Manager.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my Facebook ad active but not getting impressions?

The most common causes are the ad still being in review, a rejection, a payment issue, a budget that's too low, an audience that's too narrow, a low bid cap or the wrong schedule. The Delivery column usually tells you which one it is.

How long does Meta ad review take?

It varies by ad and by volume. Editing an ad while it's in review can send it back into review, so waiting is usually the better choice.

What can I do if my Meta ad is rejected?

Read the reason next to the ad and in Account Quality. Bring the ad in line with the policy, or request another review if you think the decision was wrong.

Does “Learning limited” mean my ad isn't delivering?

No. The ad is running, but the ad set isn't getting enough results to stabilize delivery. Merging ad sets or broadening the audience usually helps.
