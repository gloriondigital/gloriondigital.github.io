How to advertise to parents: a preschool Meta ads guide

Short answer: To advertise to parents, set up a Meta Ads Manager campaign optimized for messages or leads, limit the location to a radius around your school, build an adult audience by age and area rather than relying on “parent” interests, and run two or three ads with real school photos and video. Make sure someone answers messages quickly, then shift budget to the ad that brings visits.

“We want to run ads for our preschool, but we don't know where to start.” We hear this every enrollment season. This guide walks through how we set up school advertising to parents in Meta Ads Manager, step by step. If you want the bigger picture first, read our 4 rules for reaching the right parents with preschool ads; here we focus on the hands-on setup.

What should be ready before you advertise to parents?

Sorting out three things before launch saves budget later:

  • Accounts: your school's Facebook Page and Instagram account connected to the same Meta business account, with a payment method on the ad account. If you want messages on WhatsApp, connect your WhatsApp Business number to the Page too.
  • Offer: “Enrollment is open” is a weak message on its own. Give parents a concrete next step: a school tour, a trial day or an open house.
  • Reply plan: who answers messages, and when? Don't launch until you know.

How do you set up ads for parents, step by step?

  1. Choose the objective. If you want parents to write to you on WhatsApp, Messenger or Instagram, use the Engagement objective with messages; to collect inquiry forms, use Leads. Messaging ads take the person who clicks straight into a conversation with your business (Meta for Developers), while lead ads let people sign up for information in a few taps (Meta Business Help Center).
  2. Limit the location to your school's area. Drop a pin on your school's address instead of choosing a whole city. Meta lets you set a radius of 1 to 80 kilometers (0.63 to 50 miles) around a point (Meta for Developers). For most preschools, the few kilometers parents can travel daily is enough; widen it only if you run a bus route.
  3. Target parents by age and area, not fragile interests. Use your own enrollment data to choose the age range of parents with preschool-age children. Don't rely on “parent” detailed targeting options alone: Meta trims that list from time to time, for example removing options tied to sensitive topics from January 19, 2022 (Meta for Developers). With Advantage+ audience on, Meta may also go beyond your age and gender suggestions when it expects better performance (Meta Business Help Center), so let location do the real limiting.
  4. Keep the budget in few ad sets. Splitting a small budget across five or six ad sets makes it hard for any of them to gather enough data. Start with one campaign, one ad set and a few ads.
  5. Prepare two or three creatives. Real classroom photos, a short video of a teacher introducing herself and a parent review (shared with permission) build far more trust than stock images. Get written consent from parents for any content showing children's faces.
  6. Tie copy and button to one action. Lead with where the school is and what makes it different, then the offer, then a single call to action such as “Send message” or “Book a visit”.
  7. Set up your message flow. Prepare a greeting, saved replies for common questions and a reply to “What's your price?” that invites parents to visit. Without this, campaigns often end up among ads that get impressions but no messages.
  8. Launch and watch the first week. Early results can swing. After a week, look at cost per message or lead, the quality of conversations and how many turn into visits.

Messaging ads or lead ads?

Both work; the choice depends on how your school prefers to talk to parents:

Messaging adsLead ads
What the parent doesWrites on WhatsApp, Messenger or InstagramLeaves a name and phone number
StrengthInstant conversation, quick answersInquiries collected in one tidy list
Watch out forReplies must be fastCall people back promptly
Best forSchools that can reply during the daySchools that follow up by phone

How do you know if your parent advertising works?

Impressions and likes alone say little. In preschool accounts we look, in order, at cost per message or lead, how many conversations are real parents, how many book a visit and how many enroll. Torn between two images or two headlines? Meta's A/B testing splits the same budget so you can see which performs better.

Is the Boost button enough?

It can be enough to show one post to more people nearby, but its objective, testing and measurement options are limited. For steady messages from parents across enrollment season, we recommend setting up in Ads Manager. We explain the difference in Boost post vs Ads Manager.

Should preschools use Google Ads too?

Meta ads introduce your school to parents who haven't started searching yet. Google Ads for preschools catch parents who are already searching for things like “preschool near me”, and you can restrict them to your area with location targeting as well (Google Ads Help). On a tight budget, setting up and measuring one channel properly beats starting two halfway.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best Meta ad objective for advertising to parents?

Use Engagement with messages if you want parents to write to you, or Leads to collect inquiry forms. The Awareness objective does not optimize for messages or forms.

What radius should a preschool use when targeting parents with ads?

Meta allows a radius of 1 to 80 km (0.63 to 50 miles). For most preschools, the few kilometers parents can travel daily is enough; widen it only if you offer transport.

Can I use photos of children in preschool ads?

Only with written consent from parents for any content showing children's faces. Without it, use classroom spaces, teacher introductions and activity details that don't show faces.

When should a preschool start advertising to parents?

Start a few weeks before enrollment season with introductory content, then shift to message and lead campaigns during enrollment rather than squeezing the budget into the final week.
